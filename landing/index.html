Learn the workflow

How you build a room from your video

blender-room-tour is the workflow I used, packaged as a reusable skill for Claude Code and Codex. The agent handles Blender. Your part is to look at what it infers, compare it with your footage and ask for fixes. It’s hands-on practice in steering an agent through work outside your expertise, not a one-click 3D scan.

What you end up with

  • The blender-room-tour skill, ready to reuse on your next walkthrough.
  • An editable Blender scene, a .blend file you can open and change.
  • Review renders to compare against your footage.
  • A local viewer for exploring the room on your computer.

How a build goes

  1. 1

    Choose a walkthrough

    A video you filmed moving through a real space. The agent can only model what the footage shows.

  2. 2

    Your agent studies the footage

    It pulls reference frames, writes down the layout, openings, light and materials, and turns those notes into a Blender Python recipe.

  3. 3

    It builds the room

    From the recipe it makes the editable Blender scene, the review renders and the local viewer.

  4. 4

    You check, then ask for fixes

    Read what it inferred and compare the renders with your footage. Where something drifts, tell it what’s wrong. Some fixes take a few rounds.

Get started

You can do today

  • Pick a walkthrough video you filmed.
  • Install Blender, FFmpeg and Python 3.11+, the tools the agent will run.
  • Explore the example room to see what you’re building toward.

Technical details

Requirements

  • Claude Code or Codex
  • Node.js / npx
  • Python 3.11+
  • Blender
  • FFmpeg

Manual setup

  1. Download the skill and extract the whole blender-room-tour folder, keeping its scripts and assets together.
  2. Put it in your project. Claude Code: .claude/skills/blender-room-tour/. Codex: .agents/skills/blender-room-tour/.
  3. Ask your agent to read its SKILL.md and run doctor (doctor --stills for screened images only). It reports missing tools; it doesn’t install them.

How this started

I had no 3D modeling background. I filmed apartments I wanted to compare, then had an AI agent build each room in Blender from the footage. I checked the renders against the film and asked for fixes, over several rounds. The film tells the story in 70 seconds.
